Reviewer: 1
Comments to the Author(s)
Transposable element–host genome evolutionary arms race revealed by multi-modal epigenomic profiling in a telomere-to-telomere human genome reference - comments to the Author

The manuscript “Transposable element–host genome evolutionary arms race revealed by multi-modal epigenomic profiling in a telomere-to-telomere human genome reference” submitted by Daniil Nikitin investigates the coevolutionary epigenomic dynamics between human genome, transposable elements and associated regulatory innovation. The Author uses genomic data from the up-to-date T2T CHM13 human reference genome, along with epigenomic data for 12 cancer cell types. Broadly, methods used in this work seem sufficiently exhaustive and statistically strong. However, I have several improvement suggestions as listed below.

Major comments:
As the only one, the Author should rewrite the manuscript by using “I/My” instead of “We/Our”.

p.3-4, lines 80-102: This section’s bibliography is made up almost entirely of self-citations. I understand that the Author’s previous works are important to this manuscript, but please provide additional and external references that support your statements. Moreover, I do not believe that it is particularly ethical using such a high number of self-citations.

p.5, line 163; p.12 line 336; p.21 line 567: I would rather use “consolidate” or a synonym rather than “validate/validation”, because it sounds as a very strong and unsupported statement, since no validations such as in vitro experiments were performed.

p.8, lines 222-224: please provide evidence (consistent bibliographic references) for this statement.

p.22, lines 585-587: “Under such a framework, evolutionarily active TE families are expected to display systematic changes in occupancy by key epigenetic marks over evolutionary time, including histone modifications and DNA-binding proteins such as CTCF”. This statement is not completely understandable, since it’s not clear what occupancy should mean in this context. Therefore, I suggest rephrasing/explaining the sentence.

p.25: The last paragraph of the discussion (“3.5.Relevance of the current findings in the context of cancer and therapeutic resistance”) seems a bit too short. I strongly suggest expanding this section since all analyses were performed on cancer cells. One possibility could be discussing more deeply the different types of cancers and therapeutic resistance in the context of old and new findings.

p.27, line 826: A proper explanation of the Bowtie 2 parameters should be included and justified to explain the reason why the Author used such specific parameters.

Minor comments:
p.3, line 77: the last part of the sentence seems not to be appropriately linked to the rest, please rewrite the sentence in a more understandable way.

p.5, line 181: it should be “length-weigthed average was taken for all the metrics”.

p.12, lines 322-323: “Notably, average correlations approached negligible values (<0.05) for families exceeding 500 copies”: this is interesting, therefore I would add lines indicating the 500 copies and Spearman correlations <0.05 to highlight this result.

p.22, line 607-608: I would change “all the rest classes” with “all the other classes” and “could be in part explaned by” with “could be partially explained by”.
p.22, line 621: Are these numbers p-values from Kosuge, Ito, and Hamada 2024? It’s not clear.

p.27, line 805-807: There are too many “followed” in this sentence, please rewrite properly.

Supplementary Figure 1: “Spearman” is misspelled.

Supplementary Figure 7A: p-value is missing from LTR plot.
Supplementary Figure 7C: the plot is not entirely understandable, it would be better to add names instead of numbers on X and Y axis, and to add a color legend.

Reviewer: 2
Comments to the Author(s)
The author in this manuscript made use of the newly available T2T human reference genome and the epigenetic data from the T2T ENCODE Initiative to address a long-standing question regarding the arms race between the parasitic transposable elements and the host genome’s mechanisms in suppressing them. The work revealed a few novel observations regarding this question: 1) SVAs are shown to have the strongest signature of the arms race via progressively accumulating CTCF binding, evading H3K9me3-mediated repression, and acquiring enhancer-associated H3K4me1 in a few cell lines; 2) Alus from two very young subfamilies, AluYb8 and AluYb9, showed accumulation of CTCF binding; 3) A few LTR subfamilies demonstrated H3K9me3 and CTCF-enriched signatures. The author concludes that transposon-driven evolution is dominated by evasion of host-imposed heterochromatinization primarily at H3K9me3 and secondarily at H3K27me3, together with progressive invasion into CTCF-rich regions. These specific patterns represent novel observations in the field.

The study’s strengths are evident in its extensive data analysis using sophisticated statistical methods and results that are highly condensed. In the meantime, the manuscript is not easy to read/follow, in part because the same type of analysis and very similar type of figures were used across different parts of the results, and in part because of some issues related to figure presentation and method clarity. Not sure if it could be useful to simplify the data presentation by focusing on the most important part. The manuscript has lots of room for improvement by addressing issues, including the use of terms/phrases that are not well defined, insufficient clarity in some parts of the methods, and inconsistencies or inaccuracies in some details, as shown in the lists below.

Main issues:
• The majority of the figures and conclusions are based on Spearman's Correlation of epigenetic signals versus TE sequence divergence using heat maps and cluster maps. While I have no issue with using sequence divergence as a proxy for TEs’ age, I’m not fully convinced that the correlation between TE age and epigenetic signals has provided sufficient evidence for all the conclusions made in this manuscript.

Other critical issues:
1. L111: The total number of TEs at 4.5M is significantly different from the 3.7 M stated in the abstract and the Materials and Methods (L793). Both could be correct if they are for the number before and after integrating the fragmented ones. Clarification is needed.  
2. L380-388: I’m not convinced that all statements made in this section are clearly supported by the data in Figure 3. Some statements are quite ambiguous. For example, I’m not sure what “the highest intensity of evolutionary dynamics” specifically refers to. It would be helpful to cite the relevant part of the figure to support each statement here. BTW, the last statement seems to be self-contradicting: different cell lines from the same organ differ significantly, while cell lines from different organs show no significant differences. Can this be considered comparable degrees of biologically meaningful grouping?
3. What are the commonalities and differences between the present study and prior similar studies from the same research group, in particular, the Nikitin 2025 study?
4. How do we interpret the large difference in subfamily lists between the two studies using T2T and GRCH38, respectively (Figure 1E)? Different criteria or something else? I don’t think all can be explained by sequence differences between the two genome versions.
5. L585: Need to define “evolutionary active” of TE families.
Figures and Tables:
1. Figure 1A is never cited directly or as part of the general citing for Figure 1. Figure 1D: The colour legend doesn’t seem to match well with the colours in the figure. For example, there are dots in fresh red colour in the figure, but not in the colour legend. More contrasting colours could be used across TE classes to improve readability. The same issue is seen in Figure S1A.
2. Figure 2A: Needs to provide the used cutoff value of the Kimura divergence value in the text legend.
3. Figure 3A:  Again, the colour contrast among TE classes could be much improved by choosing more contrasting and distinct colours. In this case, the colours for LTR and SVA look identical. 3C is a bit difficult to follow as it lacks an obvious order. It can be either ordered by the TE type of feature type.
4. Table 1: The row labelled as being robust is not the one with the largest Spearman value, and it’s unclear why the other rows (i.e., 1st, 3rd and 4th) are not labelled in the same way.
5. Figure S2: Since no significant differences are observed, why are the different cutoffs used throughout in many parts?
6. Figure 4A: The values for the X-axis (TE count in a subfamily) seem to be too small (e.g., 6 as the largest). I wonder what these subfamilies are?

3. L122: Why “classified” only for subfamilies?

6. L127-142: This section could be shortened for this part of the writing.
